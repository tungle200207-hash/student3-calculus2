\section{Tendon Stress in a Cylindrical Segment}

\begin{frame}{Problem Statement and Context}

\textbf{Context:}

A cylindrical limb segment in a musculoskeletal model has a radius of \(3.5\) units and a height of \(8.5\) units. The tendon stress density at a point \((x,y,z)\) within the segment is defined by:

\[
P(x,y,z)=243.51x^2+243.51y^2
\]

The objective is to compute the \textbf{total tendon stress} in the cylindrical segment using cylindrical coordinates.

\vspace{0.4cm}

In cylindrical coordinates: $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$, $dV = r \, dr \, d\theta \, dz$.

\[
\iiint P \, dV = 243.51 \int_{0}^{8.5} dz \int_{0}^{2\pi} d\theta \int_{0}^{3.5} (r^{2}) \cdot r \, dr = 243.51 \cdot h \cdot 2\pi \int_{0}^{3.5} r^{3} \, dr
\]

\[
\int_{0}^{3.5} r^{3} \, dr = \frac{(3.5)^{4}}{4}
\]

Final result (rounded to 4 decimal places):
\[
\boxed{487896.5878}
\]
\end{frame}
